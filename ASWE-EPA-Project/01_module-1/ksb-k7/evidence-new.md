## Evidence Strategy: Compare 3 Governance Models

### Model 1: Centralized Governance (Your Proposed Approach)

**How It Works:**
- One central data governance team makes all decisions
- All data standards, policies, tools go through this team
- Other departments follow the standards

**Pros:**
- ✓ Consistent standards across organization
- ✓ Clear accountability (one team responsible)
- ✓ Easier to enforce compliance
- ✓ Simpler auditing and security

**Cons:**
- ✗ Slow decision-making (bottleneck)
- ✗ Can take 3-4 weeks to approve new dashboards
- ✗ Team feels micromanaged
- ✗ Not flexible for fast-moving teams

**Cost:**
- 3 governance staff = £150k/year
- Policy documentation = £20k setup
- Compliance tools = £30k/year
- **Total: £200k first year, £180k ongoing**

**Best For:**
- Regulated industries (finance, healthcare)
- Organizations where consistency > speed
- Large enterprises with compliance needs

---

### Model 2: Distributed Governance (Alternative)

**How It Works:**
- Each department manages their own data with loose guidelines
- Departments responsible for their own standards
- Minimal central oversight

**Pros:**
- ✓ Fast decisions (days, not weeks)
- ✓ Teams feel autonomous
- ✓ Flexible for different needs
- ✓ Lower cost

**Cons:**
- ✗ Inconsistent data definitions across departments
- ✗ Risk of duplicate tools/effort
- ✗ Security gaps (no unified control)
- ✗ Hard to share data between departments

**Cost:**
- Light oversight = £50k/year
- Tools per department = £60k/year
- Remediation of inconsistencies = £40k/year
- **Total: £150k/year**

**Best For:**
- Startup/fast-moving organizations
- Companies where speed matters more than uniformity
- Organizations with autonomous departments

---

### Model 3: Hybrid Governance (Balanced Approach)

**How It Works:**
- Central team sets standards and rules
- Departments implement within those rules
- Mix of central control + local autonomy

**Pros:**
- ✓ Balance: consistency + speed
- ✓ Teams have autonomy within guardrails
- ✓ Still compliant but not rigid
- ✓ Faster than pure centralized

**Cons:**
- ✗ More complex to manage
- ✗ Requires good communication
- ✗ Harder to enforce

**Cost:**
- 2 central staff + department contacts = £120k/year
- Tools and training = £40k/year
- **Total: £160k/year**

**Best For:**
- Mid-size organizations
- Mix of stability needs + innovation needs
- Organizations ready to scale

## Why I Chose Centralized (Simple Decision Statement)

Write a paragraph like this:

*"We evaluated three governance models: centralized (strict rules from center), distributed (each department decides), and hybrid (center sets rules, departments implement). We chose centralized governance because our organization operates in a regulated financial services environment where data errors can trigger compliance violations and financial penalties. In 2023, a data inconsistency cost us £45k in remediation. The £180k/year cost of centralized governance is justified because preventing even one major data incident (£200k+ impact) breaks even. For a startup or innovation-focused company, distributed would be better; for our context, centralized governance's consistency and compliance benefits outweigh its speed trade-off."*

## Governance Trade-offs You Considered

### Trade-off 1: Control vs Speed
*"We prioritize control over speed because compliance risk outweighs agility needs in our context. A delayed dashboard is a minor inconvenience; a data breach is catastrophic."*

### Trade-off 2: Cost vs Consistency
*"We invest in governance (£180k/year) because one prevented data incident (£200k+) pays back in Year 1. Additionally, consistent data saves 400+ staff hours/year in manual reconciliation, worth £140k."*

### Trade-off 3: Centralization vs Autonomy
*"We accept some centralization burden because distributed governance created duplicate tools (Excel in finance, Tableau in marketing, custom Python in engineering), costing £60k/year in redundant licenses. Centralized governance consolidates to Power BI, saving £40k/year net."*
