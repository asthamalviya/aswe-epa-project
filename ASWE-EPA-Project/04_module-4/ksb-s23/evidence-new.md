## Framework Comparison with Limitations

### Framework 1: Agile PM (Your Chosen Approach)

**Strengths:**
- Fast iteration, early feedback
- Flexible to changing requirements
- Team collaboration

**Limitations You'd Address:**
- Can miss big-picture planning (solving local problems vs strategic goal)
  - **Mitigation:** Quarterly strategy reviews, not just sprint goals
- Difficult for multi-team coordination
  - **Mitigation:** Scaled Agile (SAFe) for dependencies
- Documentation often neglected
  - **Mitigation:** Definition of Done includes documentation

---

### Framework 2: Waterfall PM (Alternative)

**Strengths:**
- Clear requirements upfront
- Good documentation
- Predictable timeline

**Limitations:**
- Late discovery of problems (testing at end)
  - **Cost:** If wrong, full rebuild needed
- Inflexible to change
- Long time to first value (all-or-nothing delivery)

**Why You Rejected It:** "Our organization changes priorities mid-project 40% of the time. Waterfall's inflexibility would be disaster."

---

### Framework 3: Lean PM (Alternative)

**Strengths:**
- Eliminates waste
- Fast delivery

**Limitations:**
- Can sacrifice quality for speed
- Requires experienced team (not suitable for junior staff)
- Limited governance (risky for compliance-heavy projects)

**Why You Rejected It:** "Our organization values quality over speed. Lean's speed focus doesn't match our risk culture."

## Limitations of YOUR Chosen Approach (Honest Assessment)

### Limitation 1: Agile Needs Skilled Team
- **Risk:** Junior team members get lost in fast iterations
- **Your mitigation:** Paired with senior leads, structured retrospectives, clear sprint goals
- **Evidence:** Team velocity stabilized Week 4, no critical errors

### Limitation 2: Agile Can Miss Strategic Alignment
- **Risk:** Team optimizes sprints but loses sight of 12-month strategy
- **Your mitigation:** Monthly strategy check-in, quarterly goal reset
- **Evidence:** Every sprint linked back to strategic KPIs

### Limitation 3: Documentation Often Sacrificed
- **Risk:** Knowledge lost when team members leave
- **Your mitigation:** Made documentation "Definition of Done" - no sprint closes without docs
- **Evidence:** All decisions documented, new team member onboarded in 1 week

## Organizational Learning: What Next Project Should Know

### What Worked (Replicate)
- Agile sprints with 2-week cycles (not too long, not too short)
- Weekly strategy alignment (prevented drift)
- Paired senior-junior team structure (knowledge transfer worked)

### What Didn't Work (Avoid)
- Daily standups at 9am (timezone issues for distributed team)
  - **Next project:** Async standups + 2x/week live sync
- Estimation poker (took too long, never accurate)
  - **Next project:** Use historical velocity instead

### What to Do Differently Next Time
- Start project with "Definition of Done" workshop (prevented scope creep)
- Use same retrospective facilitator (continuity, trust)
- Monthly stakeholder demos (kept sponsors engaged)
